Known Limitations

Charticks 0.1.0 · What does not work yet, and what that means for you

⚠ Your live stop lives in Charticks, not at your broker

In Live mode this build sends real orders to your broker. Stop Loss, Target and Trailing Stop Loss do now act on live positions — but they are enforced by this application, watching the price tick by tick. No stop order is resting at the exchange.

So if Charticks, your PC, or your internet connection stops, nothing is protecting the position. The stop goes away with the app.

Keep your broker's own app or website open as a backup exit. If anything fails, that is your only way out.

Automatic exits also only ever act on quantity your broker has confirmed as filled — a partially filled entry is managed at the filled size, not the size you asked for.

The specific risks

1. A live stop only exists while Charticks is running

Stop Loss, Target, Trailing Stop Loss and Portfolio Trail Profit do act on live positions. But they are enforced inside this application, which watches each tick and sends an exit order when a level is breached. Nothing is resting at the exchange.

That means a closed app, a crashed PC, a dropped internet connection or a stalled market-data feed leaves the position completely unprotected, with no warning at the exchange.

For anything you cannot afford to watch, also place a stop in your broker's own terminal. Note that if you do, and Charticks also exits, you may end up exiting twice — check your broker positions after any automatic exit.

This has been built but not yet proven on a live account. Treat the first few sessions as a test: small size, and watch what it does.

2. Roll is two separate orders, and can leave you flat

Roll now sends real orders in Live mode. (In earlier builds it did not: the app drew a rolled position that did not exist at the broker. That is fixed — nothing appears in the grid until your broker reports it.)

A roll is close the current strike, then buy the new one, and the second leg is only sent once your broker confirms the first has actually closed. That ordering is deliberate — sending both at once would double your position whenever the exit failed — but it has a consequence you should know about:

If the closing leg fills and the new leg is then refused (margin, a limit, a broker error), you end up flat rather than rolled. Charticks says so loudly in the Activity log, but it does not retry: re-entering after a rejection is your decision, not the app's. Check the Positions grid after any roll.

If the closing leg never confirms within 90 seconds the roll is abandoned and the new leg is not opened — you keep the position you had.

3. Resizing a split order

A resting live order can now be re-priced or cancelled from the app. Two caveats:

If an order was too large for one submission, Charticks split it into several broker orders. You can change the price of those (every leg is amended) and you can cancel them (every leg is cancelled), but you cannot change the quantity — one new quantity has no single meaning across legs. Cancel it and place the size you want.

A position appears in the grid only once the order actually fills, so a working order is visible in the Order Book rather than the Positions grid.

4. Duplicate protection may ask before it sends

Max Position, max trades and max loss now restrain live orders as well as paper ones — they are enforced by the engine, not the screen.

Charticks also refuses to send the same order twice by accident. If you place an identical order within two minutes of the last one, it is held and you are asked to confirm — that is deliberate, not a fault. And if a previous attempt was sent but the broker never answered, it will not resend until it has checked; if it cannot check, it asks you to look at your broker's order book first. Both prompts let you go ahead.

5. A live position takes a few seconds to appear

Live positions are read from your broker roughly every 4 seconds, so a filled order shows up shortly after — not instantly like Paper. The green “Order accepted” confirmation appears immediately with the broker's order id; the position row follows.

6. Closing a position keeps it in one row

A square-off is shown as a state of the position, not as a second row. When you close (or partially close) a position, or a Stop Loss / Target fires, that row switches to ⏳ Exit Pending and its controls lock — no editing, no Roll, no further partial exits — until your broker answers. It then either disappears (filled) or returns to normal (refused). Working entry orders still appear in the Order Book until they fill, because until then there is no position for them to be a state of.

Other things to know

Angel requires your IP to be whitelisted

Every order fails with AG7002 — “<your ip> is not a registered IP” unless your machine's public IP is registered on your own SmartAPI app. On mobile tethering or a CGNAT connection your IP changes without warning, which breaks order placement mid-session. See BROKER-SETUP.html.

The holiday calendar is a file, and it is only as current as you keep it

Charticks now knows the exchange trading holidays and refuses orders on one, naming the holiday. The dates live in resources/sidecar/config/market_holidays.json and are seeded for 2026 only. They must be refreshed each year from the NSE and MCX circulars. If the file is missing, unreadable or out of date, the app falls back to treating every weekday as a trading day — the old behaviour — and your broker rejects the order instead.

Half-days and special sessions (Muhurat, an MCX evening-only session on an equity holiday) are not modelled. Your broker remains the authority on the exact minute.

Paper positions are not saved when the app restarts

Paper positions and order history are held in memory only; closing the app or restarting the engine clears them.

Live positions are different. The live book — including every Stop Loss, Target and Trailing Stop — is written to disk on every change and restored on restart. It is then re-confirmed against your broker's own position book before any automation acts on it, so a stop is never fired against a position that was closed while Charticks was not running.

Quitting stops your stops — and now says so

Because live stops are enforced by this app, closing it removes them. Charticks now warns before it closes if it is managing live positions, and lists them. It does not square them off for you: closing your book is your decision. If you quit anyway, your broker still holds the positions and nothing is watching them.

Adjusting lots on a live position is not supported

The AdjLots control works in Paper only. To add to a live position, buy or sell it again from the Option Chain; to reduce it, use the partial-exit buttons. Both place real broker orders. (In Live mode the control is absent rather than inert.)

Notifications are saved but not raised

The Notifications card in Settings stores your preferences and is marked "not yet active": this version raises no desktop or sound alerts. Fills, modifications, SL / Target hits and connection changes all appear in the Activity log and in logs/orders.log.

A hedge belongs to the trade it protects

An automatic hedge is not treated as a separate trade. Charticks records which short it was opened for, and the Positions grid labels both sides (🛡 Hedge on the protective leg, 🛡 Hedged on the short). The link is saved to disk, so it survives a restart.

When the last short a hedge was protecting closes — whether you closed it here or in your broker's own app — Charticks asks whether to close the hedge or keep it. It never decides for you: closing it is an exit you did not request, and keeping it silently leaves a long position you did not choose to hold on its own. If the same hedge is covering more than one short, closing one of them changes nothing and you are not asked.

Auto Hedge protects a short, and reports it when it cannot

With Auto Hedge on, a confirmed short fill is followed by a protective long leg the configured distance further out of the money. It is placed by the engine on the fill (not on the order being accepted) and carries no stop of its own — a stop on a hedge would take the protection off and leave the short naked. If the hedge cannot be placed, the Activity log says so as an error and the short is left plainly unhedged; it is never silently skipped.

Closing an ICICI session key expires daily

ICICI issues a session key by browser login that dies each day. Reconnect that account every morning before trading.

The app is not code-signed

Windows will warn on install. See INSTALL.html.

Use Paper mode for anything you are still evaluating. It runs on the same live market feed and the same execution rules as live trading — market orders fill at the ask, sells at the bid, limit orders rest until the market reaches them. The only difference is that no order leaves your machine.
